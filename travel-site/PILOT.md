# Pilot trip kit

Goal of the first 2-3 trips: learn what breaks, and leave with real photos and reviews. Keep each group small (6-10), charge honestly, and tell
guests it is an early trip. Nothing below is legal advice; confirm vehicle, permit and insurance rules for your routes yourself.

## 1. Before you sell: confirm every supplier in writing (WhatsApp is fine)
For each hotel/homestay, vehicle owner and activity operator, save a message that states: dates, number of people, what is included, the price you
agreed, the cancellation terms, and a phone number for the day. If they will not put it in writing, do not build the trip on them.
- Vehicle: licence and permit are valid for passenger hire (ask to see them), driver's name and number, pickup time, and who pays tolls and parking.
- Stays: room count and bed types match your group, check-in and check-out times, and whether they have a backup if they overbook.
- Activities: operator name, safety briefing, age or health limits, and what happens in bad weather.
Your site promises "tested timings" only for routes you have actually travelled; do not stretch that to a new route.

## 2. Money
Take the advance to your business account only (never a personal-looking UPI that does not match your legal name). Send written terms before
the advance: what is included, what is not, the refund rule (match `refunds/` on the site), and the date the balance is due. Keep a simple sheet:
guest, amount received, date, what it covers, what you owe each supplier.

## 3. Messages (edit the brackets, send from your WhatsApp Business number)
**On booking:** "Hi [name], your [route] trip on [dates] is confirmed. Included: [list]. Not included: [list]. Balance of [amount] due by [date]. Cancellation: [rule]. Meeting point: [place, time]. I'll add you to the trip group 3 days before."

**48 hours before (trip group):** "Hi all! Final details for [route]: meet at [place] at [time] (arrive 15 min early). Vehicle: [type, number] / driver [name, phone]. Weather: [forecast]. Carry: ID proof, [clothes], medicines, power bank, cash for small shops. Tell me today about allergies, health issues or anyone running late. My number on the day: [number]."

**Each morning of the trip:** today's plan, pickup time, one thing to carry, and who to call if you cannot be reached.

**Last evening (review request, with permission):** "Thank you for travelling with us! If you enjoyed it, reply with a line or two about the trip. We'd love to show it on our website with your first name and the trip (for example 'Rohan K., 14-person Rajasthan trip'). Reply YES if that is okay. Photos you are happy for us to use: send them here, and tell us if anyone should be left out."
Keep each reply: it is your written consent. Never edit a quote beyond obvious typos; never write one yourself.

## 4. Day-of checklist
- Confirm vehicle and driver the night before and again on the morning.
- Head count at every stop; know where the nearest hospital and pharmacy are for each day.
- Share the live plan and your number with one person who is not on the trip.
- Photo etiquette: ask before photographing faces, and never post a guest's photo without their reply to the consent message.
- Note timings that were wrong (drive times, queues, opening hours). Update the plan afterwards; this is what makes the blueprint truthful.

## 5. If something goes wrong
1. Safety first: medical issue or accident means call emergency services (112 in India), then the nearest hospital, then tell the group.
2. Vehicle breakdown or supplier no-show: call the supplier, then your backup (arrange one for each day beforehand).
3. Tell guests plainly what happened and what you are doing; do not promise what you cannot deliver.
4. Write down what happened, what it cost, and what you refunded or compensated, the same day.

## 6. After the trip
- Within 48 hours: thank-you message, balance sheet check, pay suppliers.
- Add approved reviews to `brand.json` (see LAUNCH.md, "After every trip") and approved photos with `tools/add_route_photo.py`.
- One page of notes: what to change before the next trip. Then decide what to charge, using your real costs and what guests said was fair.
